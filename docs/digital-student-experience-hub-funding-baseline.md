This reduces the Year 1 architecture line from £54,016 to **£40,512**, a **£13,504** reduction. Years 2 and 3 remain at the previous 1.0 FTE high-water level until the enduring architecture model is agreed.

## Recruitment and Year 1 mobilisation

Jon Ward advises that even if recruitment for all new roles begins on day one, **around six months is a realistic recruitment period**, with appointments onboarding steadily rather than all becoming available together.

The current business case should **not automatically discount the remaining Year 1 envelope because recruitment will take time**. Contractor cover could increase cash cost, while using existing capacity creates an opportunity cost elsewhere. The next Digital and IT pass should translate the six-month assumption into a month-by-month recruitment, onboarding and bridge plan before final Year 1 phasing is submitted.

For architecture specifically, James has now supplied enough detail to phase the budget. The Solution Architect / Technical Lead should average **0.75 FTE in Year 1**, with heavier input at the start of mobilisation and reducing input as architecture stabilises. This is a Year 1 phasing decision only; it does not yet reduce the later-year or BAU high-water architecture exposure.

## D365 / Power Platform contingency

The previous 1.0 FTE Dynamics Developer line should now be understood as a **D365 / Power Platform contingency**, not a settled dedicated Student Hub role.

James describes the original requirement as deliberately prudent and currently difficult to define. D365 / Power Platform work may be needed for specific Student Hub or King’s Edge MVP capabilities, but some or all of that work may be deliverable through the **core R&C CRM team**. The current cash provision remains **£44,160 in Year 1 and £66,240 in each full later year** until the MVP clarifies the actual requirement.

The next decision should therefore start from the MVP rather than from the technology: identify which concrete student-facing capabilities require D365 / Power Platform, then determine whether they need dedicated Hub capacity, fractional support, existing CRM-team capacity or no additional D365 provision at all.

**Student-facing AI inference, retrieval and token consumption** remains at the same provisional **£50k / £75k / £100k** ramp and **£100k per year in BAU**. James also considers this a reasonable high-water assumption. The next step is to turn it into a usage and unit-cost model as the service design becomes clearer.

**Devin/Cognition remains £90k per year** as a separate engineering-productivity allowance. James expects one 4,000-ACU instance to provide the team with a meaningful amount of AI-first development capacity. Sharing should be revisited from actual ACU consumption and renewal evidence rather than assumed in advance.

**Infuse QA/testing remains confirmed non-pay outsourced service expenditure** at **£6k per month**, giving **£48k in Year 1** and **£72k per full year** thereafter. Its value includes test automation and non-functional testing expertise.

Development software remains a possible shared Student/Staff Hub category and should be explicitly allocated when James can identify which tooling is genuinely common.

## Current questions for Digital and IT

The Product Manager allocation, Infuse treatment and Year 1 architecture average are now decisions. Azure, student-facing AI and the single Devin instance are reasonable high-water assumptions rather than unsupported placeholders. The remaining handback is:

1. **Recruitment and Year 1 bridge:** What month-by-month deployment should we assume across the six-month recruitment period, and how should any gap be bridged between contractors and reprioritised existing capacity?
2. **D365 / Power Platform and MVP:** Which concrete Student Hub / King’s Edge MVP capabilities actually require D365 or Power Platform, and can those requirements be delivered through the core R&C CRM team?
3. **Architecture after Year 1:** What should the Solution Architect / Technical Lead profile be after Year 1 and in BAU?
4. **DevOps / infrastructure / IAM:** James believes DevOps may be reducible. What lower average is credible, and which specialist inputs can be supplied through standing/shared Digital and IT capacity?
5. **Shared development software:** Which development and engineering tools support both Student and Staff Hubs, and how should those costs be allocated?
6. **Operating-cost evidence:** As architecture and demand become clearer, convert the accepted Azure and student-facing AI high-water envelopes into consumption and unit-cost forecasts.
7. **Devin utilisation:** Track actual use of the 4,000-ACU instance and revisit sharing at renewal only if evidence supports it.

## Planning principle

The business case should continue to carry uncertain requirements at high-water until a named and credible alternative is agreed. Confirmed reductions should be applied immediately and visibly. The next major reduction opportunity is now likely to come from **MVP-led D365 clarification and specialist-service sourcing**, rather than from arbitrarily trimming the permanent delivery core.
