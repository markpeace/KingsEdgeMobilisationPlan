## Purpose

This document provides a portfolio-level investment view of the King’s Edge mobilisation plan for prioritisation and investment conversations. It groups related deliverables into a small number of strategic investment packages and translates the underlying plan into an accessible account of investment, benefit and enduring institutional change.

It is a **derived decision-support view, not a new source of truth**. Deliverable scope, benefits, resources, dependencies and costs remain owned by the underlying mobilisation-plan data. This document should be refreshed when those sources materially change.

It is intentionally **not surfaced through the King’s Edge plan interface or main navigation**. It exists as a shareable working view for portfolio discussion.

Deliverable **2.3.3 is currently deferred** and is excluded from the active package view unless that decision changes.

## Logic behind the grid

### Strategic packages

Packages group deliverables that collectively purchase a coherent institutional change. They do not replace the underlying project and deliverable structure. The package is the level at which the portfolio can ask: **what strategic change are we buying, what does the first investment unlock, what is the full mobilisation exposure, and what enduring liability are we creating?**

### Plan refs

Every package remains traceable to the deliverables that underpin it. Dependencies may cross package boundaries, but a dependency is not automatically rebilled into every package that relies on it.

### North Star

The North Star describes the enduring institutional condition the package is intended to create. It is deliberately different from the Year 1 return: Year 1 normally establishes, tests or proves part of the route to the North Star rather than claiming the destination has already been reached.

### What changes for people

This column translates the **benefits already defined in the underlying deliverables** into concise first-person user stories. It is not a separate benefits framework.

The personas should follow the actual beneficiaries of the package rather than a standard template. They may include students, course teams, academics, service owners, Digital and IT, recruitment or reputation colleagues, employers, alumni, external partners, prospective students and **The University** where the material benefit is genuinely institutional.

A package can have as many user stories as are useful to express the material range of benefits. It does not need one story for every possible user group, and it should not force Student / Academic / Professional Services symmetry where the underlying benefits do not support it.

The stories should remain short, concrete and experiential. Collectively they should answer: **if this package succeeds, what becomes meaningfully different for the people or institution that benefit from it?**

### Year 1 investment & return

This combines the incremental investment required in 2026/27 with the concrete institutional position that investment buys. It should make the first investment decision legible without implying that the whole transformation is complete.

The preferred grammar is broadly: **£X invested → King’s has established / tested / launched / proved Y, creating the basis to scale Z.**

### Estimated mobilisation investment

This describes the currently identifiable investment across the three-year mobilisation. Where later costs are intentionally evidence-led, the grid should say so rather than manufacture false precision.

### Likely BAU liability

This makes permanent or recurrent commitments visible separately from temporary mobilisation expenditure. The purpose is to avoid approving apparently time-limited investment while obscuring the institutional capacity or operating cost that must remain after mobilisation.

### Costing rules

- The grid presents **cash values**, not FTE-only resource descriptions.
- Direct cash investment is included once.
- Funded shared capacity is converted into its cash value and apportioned once according to the package’s share of that resource.
- Existing institutional capacity already paid for is not presented as new incremental investment.
- Shared dependencies can support several packages, but their cost is not duplicated.
- Where the plan specifies an FTE requirement but not a cash value, any conversion used in this view must be clearly labelled as a planning assumption until an agreed benchmark or authored cost replaces it.
- Mobilisation-only resource is not automatically treated as BAU.
- BAU liabilities remain visible even where their enduring organisational home or funding route is still to be agreed.

### Source-of-truth rule

**When this package view and the underlying plan diverge, the underlying plan wins.** Refresh this document rather than altering source material merely to make the package table reconcile.

## Current costing notes and assumptions

### Digital Student Experience Hub

The current **£358k Year 1 baseline** comprises:

- approximately **£203k** for the four experienced product-team posts in the Year 1 planning period;
- **£90k** for agentic software-development tooling;
- **£50k** for Azure, environments and platform costs; and
- **£15k** for paid student visioning and co-design internships.

The current **c.£394k p.a. BAU baseline** comprises approximately **£304k** for the permanent four-post experienced product core plus **£90k p.a.** for agentic tooling. Other future Hub integration and platform costs are intentionally expected to be costed from live evidence and the approved roadmap rather than carried forward as assumed annual envelopes.

The current **at least c.£1.216m mobilisation figure** is a quantified floor, not a fixed three-year cap. It reflects the presently quantified product capability/tooling and initial platform/co-design commitments; later journey integration and platform investment remains evidence-led.

### Graduate Futures Intelligence and Value

The current **£148.2k Year 1 planning total** is built from:

- **£70.7k direct activity**, comprising £30k for 2.4.1, £15k for 2.4.2, £25k for 2.4.4 and £0.7k direct LLM cost for 2.1.1;
- **£33k planning conversion for the dedicated 0.5 FTE 2.4.4 role**, using a £66k fully-loaded annual benchmark because the current source specifies the FTE requirement but not an authored cash value; and
- **£44.5k apportioned shared funded capacity**, comprising £17.5k analytics, £10.5k Project Officer and £16.5k programme/project-management capacity.

The **c.£166k p.a. BAU figure is a current planning proxy**, not a settled recurrent budget. It currently assumes approximately £66k for a 1.0 FTE activation/reputation role, a £75k mature activation/research envelope and a £25k external-insight operating envelope. The enduring analytical, content-refresh and organisational model should be settled from mobilisation evidence.

The **c.£736k mobilisation total is a current package-level planning estimate** and should be refreshed when constituent deliverables or shared-resource allocations change.

## Maintenance checklist

Before using this grid for a formal investment discussion:

1. Refresh the constituent deliverables from the default branch.
2. Refresh `src/data/shared-resources.json` and confirm supersession/reconciliation rules.
3. Recalculate each package’s direct cash and cash-converted share of funded shared capacity.
4. Confirm that dependencies have not been double counted across packages.
5. Confirm that Year 1 wording describes the actual first institutional return rather than the eventual North Star.
6. Check the user stories against the current benefit statements and beneficiaries in the source deliverables.
7. Keep estimates, proxies and deliberately evidence-led future costs visibly qualified.
8. Confirm that any BAU liability created during mobilisation is shown even if its enduring funding route remains unresolved.
