## Purpose

This is the portfolio-level investment handover for King’s Edge. It combines the four-area investment architecture, the recommended Core baseline, explicit Economy and Enhanced service variants, the in-year resource profile and the accounting boundaries needed for the outline business case.

**Core remains the recommended case and the source-plan baseline.** Economy and Enhanced are decision-support variants. They do not overwrite the canonical deliverable resource profiles unless an investment decision changes the source plan.

The investment case uses four connected investment areas:

1. **King’s Architecture for Employability**
2. **Experiential Learning in the Curriculum**
3. **Beyond-Course Opportunity and Participation**
4. **Graduate Futures Intelligence and Value**

This is an investment-grouping layer only. It does not alter the canonical 4 x 4 King’s Edge delivery architecture or merge the underlying deliverables.

The **Digital Student Experience Hub remains a separate institutional investment**. The **Student Opportunities Fund award pot remains outside the King’s Edge ask** unless a separate approved funding route is brought into scope.

All grade-based `2.2.2` staffing figures are treated as **fully loaded planning costs including employer National Insurance and pension / superannuation**, subject to Finance and HR validation.

## The investment story

King’s Edge is an investment in a developmental architecture for a King’s education. It connects purpose, disciplinary learning, capabilities, experience, relationships, evidence and transition around the individual student, while building the institutional machinery needed to make that proposition real at scale.

### What King’s means by employability

At King’s, employability is broader than preparation for employment. It is the capacity to understand what matters, develop and apply disciplinary and wider capabilities, build relationships and experience, evidence learning credibly and make purposeful transitions beyond the degree.

Positive employment and further-study outcomes are important consequences of that architecture, but they are not its only educational purpose. The proposition should not be reduced to job-search preparation, generic skills training or a conventional Careers service expansion.

### The four investment areas

| Strategic role | King’s Edge investment area | What it changes |
|---|---|---|
| **Build the architecture and infrastructure** | **King’s Architecture for Employability** | Connects purpose, skills, evidence, opportunity, relationships, global experience and transition, supported by the people and operating infrastructure required to make the model work at institutional scale. |
| **Change curriculum and near-curriculum practice** | **Experiential Learning in the Curriculum** | Makes experiential learning a deliberate educational proposition, with a future entitlement, evidence-led curriculum growth and reusable academic, regulatory and delivery routes. |
| **Grow and steward opportunity around the course** | **Beyond-Course Opportunity and Participation** | Actively shapes a richer, more inclusive opportunity ecology using commissioning, participation evidence and a shared student-year rhythm. |
| **Learn, improve and evidence value** | **Graduate Futures Intelligence and Value** | Shows where graduate value is strong or uneven, uses evidence and external challenge to strengthen it, and turns credible evidence into student articulation, outcomes improvement and external recognition. |

The boundary between the first three areas is deliberate. A relationship may be sourced and developed through the **King’s Architecture for Employability**, then produce a live curriculum project through **Experiential Learning in the Curriculum** or a flexible opportunity through **Beyond-Course Opportunity and Participation**.

## Area 1: King’s Architecture for Employability

### Core thesis

King’s establishes a distinctive architecture for employability that connects purpose, disciplinary learning, capabilities, experience, relationships, evidence and transition around the individual student, underpinned by the people, partnerships and institutional infrastructure needed to make that model work at scale.

A simple student journey is:

**What matters to me → what am I developing → where can I apply it → who can I connect with → how can I evidence it → what happens next?**

The area combines three connected components:

- **Purpose, capability and evidence:** `2.2.1`, `2.1.3`, `2.4.3`.
- **Opportunity and relationship infrastructure:** `2.2.2` common operating model, UK opportunity development, international employer engagement and Global Mobility.
- **Transition into graduate futures:** `2.2.2` Graduate Transitions capability.

The Core area combines the existing **£252k Purpose / Skills / Recognition** mobilisation package with the Core `2.2.2` profile of **£1.192551m**, giving **£1.444551m** over the three-year window.

### Economy service model

Economy protects the immediate capability judged necessary to avoid losing existing international activity, but deliberately narrows the later build.

**2026/27, same protected base as Core**
- G6 Employer Relations & Insights Manager (International) from October 2026.
- G5 East Asia Adviser from January 2027.
- G6 Global Mobility Opportunity Development Manager from January 2027.
- £15k international employer-facing / in-country non-pay.

**From 2027/28**
- Lean **three-post UK team**: 1 x G6 Experiential Partnerships & Opportunity Development Manager, 1 x G5 Experiential Opportunity Development Adviser, 1 x G5 Experiential Partnerships Operations & Intelligence Adviser.
- International capability remains **G6 + East Asia G5 only**. The South/South East Asia and Europe/North America G5 posts are not mobilised.
- Global Mobility remains **G6 only**. The G5 opportunity-development officer is not mobilised.
- International non-pay is **£35k p.a.** rather than £50k.

**From September 2028**
- One **G6 Graduate Transitions Coach** is added under existing leadership rather than a new G7 + G6 team.

**Economy consequences**
- lower UK opportunity-development throughput and less faculty-facing brokerage capacity;
- international employer development concentrated on East Asia rather than three regional portfolios;
- less capacity to grow shorter-form and work-related outward mobility;
- graduate-transition support has lower reach and less dedicated leadership;
- greater dependence on existing teams to absorb complexity and prioritisation.

### Core service model, recommended

Core is the current source-plan baseline.

- International G6 from October 2026 and East Asia G5 from January 2027.
- Global Mobility G6 from January 2027.
- Four-post UK team from September 2027: 1 x G6 manager, 2 x G5 opportunity-development advisers, 1 x G5 operations/intelligence adviser.
- Global Mobility G5 from September 2027.
- £50k p.a. international non-pay from 2027/28.
- Graduate Transitions G7 + G6 from September 2028.
- Final two international regional G5s from January 2029.

This gives King’s a balanced institutional model with dedicated opportunity creation, operational support, three international regional portfolios, two Global Mobility opportunity-development posts and a small proactive Graduate Transitions team.

### Enhanced service model

Enhanced accelerates capacity so that higher opportunity commissioning and implementation ambition are supported by greater relationship and transition infrastructure.

**2026/27**
- Core international G6 and East Asia G5 phasing is retained.
- **Both Global Mobility posts** start from January 2027, rather than only the G6.
- International non-pay increases to **£25k**.

**From September 2027**
- **Five-post UK team**: the Core four-post model plus a third G5 Experiential Opportunity Development Adviser.
- The final two international regional G5s are brought forward from January 2029 to **September 2027**, so the full international team is live in Year 2.
- The full **G7 + G6 Graduate Transitions team** is brought forward from September 2028 to **September 2027**.
- International non-pay rises to **£75k p.a.**

**Enhanced consequences**
- greater faculty-facing opportunity-development throughput;
- full international market coverage 16 months earlier than Core;
- earlier capacity to grow purposeful outward mobility;
- proactive graduate-transition capability available a year earlier;
- more credible operational capacity behind the much larger Enhanced opportunity-commissioning portfolio.

## Decision framing

The option decision can now be stated plainly:

- **Economy, £2.904m:** King’s establishes the architecture but deliberately limits geographic coverage, opportunity-development throughput, Global Mobility growth and graduate-transition capacity.
- **Core, £3.583m:** King’s funds a balanced institutional model capable of delivering the proposition credibly at scale. **Recommended.**
- **Enhanced, £4.852m:** King’s accelerates international coverage, Global Mobility, Graduate Transitions and UK opportunity-development capacity, matching a materially larger opportunity and implementation portfolio.

The main approval question is therefore no longer whether to apply an arbitrary percentage reduction or uplift to the same model. It is **which level of institutional capability King’s wants to establish during the three-year mobilisation window, and what coverage and throughput it is willing to trade away or accelerate.**
