# King’s Edge portfolio investment handover

> **Working decision-support view**  
> **Primary audience:** Mark Peace and ESS Portfolio colleagues  
> **Status:** Single King’s Edge investment handover combining strategic proposition, Core baseline and Economy / Core / Enhanced options; Digital Student Experience Hub shown separately  
> **Last updated:** 7 September 2026

## Purpose

This is the single portfolio-level investment handover for King’s Edge. It combines the strategic proposition, North Stars, user-story hypotheses, Year 1 transformations and package costs with explicit **Economy / Core / Enhanced** investment choices and an indicative model of what those choices mean for student reach, student-hours and depth of opportunity.

**Core is the recommended case and the source-plan baseline.** Economy and Enhanced are decision-support variants around it, not alternative source plans. The underlying deliverable resource profiles remain authored on the Core basis unless an investment decision changes that baseline.

The **Digital Student Experience Hub remains a separate institutional investment**. It is not part of the King’s Edge funding ask, although King’s Edge is its first substantive native product domain and depends on it for enduring digital implementation.

The **Student Opportunities Fund award pot is outside all three King’s Edge asks**. King’s Edge may design and test the operating proposition, but any new award pot requires a separate institutional, consolidated-fund, philanthropic or other funding route.

## Executive handover: the investment story

King’s Edge is an investment in a **new developmental architecture for a King’s education**. It connects purpose, curriculum, wider opportunity, evidence and recognition around the individual student and gives King’s the evidence and institutional capability to keep improving that experience and demonstrate the resulting graduate value.

The four King’s Edge investment packages are parts of one educational system:

| Strategic role | King’s Edge package | What it changes |
|---|---|---|
| **Shape and Claim** | **Student Purpose, Skills and Educational Recognition** | Students can explore what matters to them, understand and evidence what they are developing, and carry a richer trusted account of their learning forward. |
| **Build within and near the course** | **Experiential Learning in the Curriculum** | Experiential learning becomes a deliberate educational proposition, with evidence-led curriculum growth and reusable recognised routes rather than isolated local practice. |
| **Build around the course** | **Beyond-Course Opportunity and Participation** | King’s actively stewards a richer, more inclusive opportunity ecology using evidence, partnerships, commissioning and a shared rhythm. |
| **Learn and Carry** | **Graduate Futures Intelligence and Value** | King’s understands where graduate value is strong or uneven, uses evidence and external challenge to strengthen it, and turns credible evidence into student articulation, outcomes improvement and external recognition. |

The recommended Core case deliberately protects Year 1 discovery, evidence and live testing while avoiding large later-year commitments that should only be released once evidence exists. It also protects the **£150k / £200k / £250k Beyond-Course opportunity commissioning trajectory**, because this is the clearest direct route from additional investment to additional student experience.

## What the options mean for students: indicative opportunity modelling

The most directly quantifiable difference between the options is the **Beyond-Course Opportunity Commissioning and Growth** portfolio. The Core source plan already models three things together:

1. **annual distinct student reach**;
2. **total student-hours**, as a better proxy for the amount of developmental experience purchased than headcount alone;
3. **sustained high-intensity places**, including deeper projects, placements, mentoring and comparable experiences.

The portfolio deliberately mixes **reusable evergreen provision**, **short active experiences** and **sustained intensive opportunities**. This means benefits do **not** scale linearly with cash. Reusable assets allow reach to grow at relatively low marginal cost, while partnership capacity and people-intensive delivery matter disproportionately for total student-hours and the sustained layer.

### What changes in the intensity balance

The modelling suggests that Economy can recover much of Core’s **headline reach** by Years 2 and 3 because the direct commissioning envelope catches back up and reusable provision accumulates. What it does not recover as readily is **depth**: lower partnership capacity, fewer Student Life interventions and greater dependence on existing teams constrain the number of people-intensive experiences the institution can operate well.

Using the midpoint of the scenario ranges as an indicative intensity check:

| Year 3 indicator | **Economy** | **Core** | **Enhanced** |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Annual reach | c.6,250 | 6,500 | c.9,500 |
| Student-hours per reached student | **c.14.8 hrs** | **c.16.5 hrs** | **c.19.5 hrs** |
| Sustained high-intensity places as a share of annual reach | **c.7%** | **c.9%** | **c.12%** |

This is the central student-facing distinction:

- **Economy is increasingly breadth-first.** It can still reach a large population, but more of the portfolio sits in evergreen and short-active forms and fewer students progress into sustained experiences.
- **Core protects a deliberate balance of breadth and depth.** Reach grows substantially while the sustained layer remains meaningful.
- **Enhanced grows breadth and depth together.** The extra investment is deliberately used to avoid a model in which a larger headline reach simply means more lightweight participation.

Across the three mobilisation years, the opportunity portfolio would generate approximately:

| | Student-hours across mobilisation | Sustained high-intensity place-years |
|---|---:|---:|
| **Economy** | **c.146–174k** | **c.925–1,125** |
| **Core** | **c.181.5–213.5k** | **c.1,450** |
| **Enhanced** | **c.294–355k** | **c.2,175–2,525** |

These totals deliberately do not sum annual reach into a three-year “unique students” figure, because the same student may participate in more than one year.

A useful way to describe the Enhanced case is that by Year 3 it could reach roughly the **Core plan’s eventual steady-state scale** of around 9,000 students, but with materially greater depth: roughly **170–200k student-hours and 1,000–1,200 sustained places**, compared with the Core steady-state planning position of around 145–165k hours and 600 sustained places.

The institution buys a genuinely larger and deeper opportunity ecology rather than simply more enrolments. |
| **Partnership capacity and external opportunity creation** | More brokerage, contracting and partner support remains with existing faculty and professional-service teams. Partnership capacity is more likely to limit the sustained layer and to vary by faculty. | Creates a credible central and faculty-facing partnership engine while still expecting meaningful reuse of existing institutional capability. | Creates capacity for more faculties, more complex opportunities and a larger external network, reducing the risk that placements, projects and community/employer activity are constrained by fragmented operating capacity. |

Successful participation interventions depend more heavily on local adoption. | Provides enough live testing and scale funding to make the Student Life by Design approach visible beyond isolated pilots. | Successful models can operate across more campuses, places and student moments, giving the institution more capacity to respond deliberately to participation gaps. |
| **Purpose, skills and educational recognition** | The architecture can still be discovered and tested, but across fewer contexts. Institutionalisation is slower and more dependent on strong local adopters. | Tests the propositions deeply across a bounded set of contexts and funds the elements that survive portability testing. | Supports a broader trailblazer footprint across disciplines and student-facing settings, generating stronger portability evidence and a faster route into mainstream practice. |
| **Experiential learning in the curriculum** | King’s still gets the common educational model, curriculum baseline, sandwich-year route and shared capstone, but **most implementation has to happen through existing faculty and curriculum capacity**. Central resource can actively support only the highest-priority gaps, so uneven access is likely to reduce more slowly and strong local capacity matters more. | King’s can **actively intervene in identified weak spots**, moving a bounded set of programmes from diagnosis into changed curriculum while proving reusable academic, policy and delivery models. Mainstream curriculum mechanisms still carry most change, but Core buys enough central implementation capacity to demonstrate that the model works beyond guidance alone. | King’s can use mobilisation as a **curriculum-implementation accelerator**, proactively supporting a materially wider cohort of programmes or faculties rather than waiting for local capacity. This buys faster reduction in uneven access, more disciplinary exemplars and a larger reusable implementation library inside the mobilisation period. |
| **Graduate Futures, outcomes and reputation** | Graduate Futures remains primarily an **intelligence and targeted-intervention function**. King’s keeps the evidence engine and can identify priority issues, but has to choose more tightly between student activation, strategic external relationships, survey optimisation and reputation work rather than running them all with sustained intensity. | Creates an **integrated annual improvement cycle** in which course-level evidence, external challenge, student articulation, Graduate Outcomes optimisation and targeted reputation activity can consistently feed one another. This is enough capacity to turn intelligence into repeatable institutional action rather than primarily producing insight. | Creates an **active institutional graduate-value function** with enough sustained capacity to run course improvement, student adoption, outcomes optimisation, employer/alumni engagement and external reputation work in parallel at greater scale. King’s can pursue multiple opportunities simultaneously rather than continually sequencing them around limited capacity. |
| **Institutional consistency and delivery risk** | Higher risk of **partial institutionalisation**. By 2029 King’s may have strong models and evidence, but less consistency across faculties and campuses and more reliance on local champions. | Recommended balance of new capacity and evidence gates: enough resource to reach a visibly different institutional position without overbuilding ahead of proof. | Buys pace, breadth and resilience, with more of the transformation visible across the institution inside the mobilisation period. The trade-off is a larger recurrent operating model and greater risk of creating capacity ahead of demonstrated demand. |

In plain terms:

> **Economy preserves the architecture and can recover much of eventual headline reach, but relies more heavily on existing capacity: beyond-course participation is lighter-touch, curriculum implementation is selective, and Graduate Futures operates primarily as an intelligence and targeted-intervention function. Core protects a credible balance of breadth, depth and institutionalisation across all four packages. Enhanced buys substantially more reach and depth, but also faster curriculum adoption, a broader purpose/skills/recognition footprint and a genuinely active institutional graduate-value function.**

## Accounting boundaries and uncertainty

- **Core is the source-plan baseline.** Economy and Enhanced remain scenario overlays unless an investment decision changes the source plan.
- The Core Beyond-Course benefits model is now explicitly retained in 2.2.4: **2,500 / 4,000 / 6,500 annual distinct students; 26.5–33.5k / 55–65k / 100–115k student-hours; c.350 / 500 / 600 sustained high-intensity places** across Years 1–3.
- Economy and Enhanced benefit ranges are **indicative scenario modelling**, not promises. They should be recalibrated from actual delivery evidence and the eventual thematic mix.
- The **£400k Core partnership-infrastructure figure is a planning assumption**, not an approved recurrent settlement. Year 1 must still be capable of recommending a lower or higher operating model.
- **Student Opportunities Fund awards are excluded from all three King’s Edge asks.**
- Digital product implementation for purpose, skills and recognition sits in the **separate Digital Student Experience Hub** and is not rebilled into King’s Edge.
- Partnership infrastructure is costed once in Beyond Course even where it enables curricular experiential learning.
- The Strategic Partnerships, Profile & External Engagement Fund remains costed once in 2.4.4 and can be deployed across King’s Edge priorities.
- Any approved **£100k September–December acceleration bridge is a timing mechanism, not an additional layer of programme funding**. Spend must be reconciled against later approved Year 1 budgets, and any 4.1.2 Hub co-design spend remains attributed to the separate Digital Student Experience Hub investment.
- Economy / Core / Enhanced BAU figures are planning exposures, not approved recurrent budgets. TBC operating requirements can move them in either direction.

## Recommended decision framing for ESS Portfolio

The investment decision is a choice about **how much student opportunity, depth and institutional scaling King’s wants to buy, and how much delivery risk it is willing to absorb through existing capacity**.

- **Economy:** prove the whole architecture and preserve broad eventual reach, but accept fewer student-hours and sustained experiences, selective curriculum implementation, slower purpose/skills/recognition institutionalisation, and a Graduate Futures function that must prioritise tightly between insight and activation.
- **Core:** fund the recommended balance of broad reach, meaningful depth and institutionalisation across all four packages: enough capacity to intervene in priority curriculum gaps, test portability of purpose/skills/recognition, run an integrated Graduate Futures improvement cycle and sustain a credible opportunity engine.
- **Enhanced:** accelerate beyond Core across the whole architecture: substantially more and deeper Beyond-Course participation, a wider curriculum implementation cohort, broader disciplinary adoption of purpose/skills/recognition, and an active institutional graduate-value function able to pursue improvement, student adoption and external reputation in parallel.

Later-year envelopes should remain subject to evidence gates. Selecting a higher envelope creates authority to scale where evidence supports it, not an obligation to spend regardless of performance.
