# King's Edge Theory of Change

**Status: Working hypothesis**  
**Last updated: 31 July 2026**

## Purpose of this document

This document holds the emerging programme-level theory of change for King's Edge.

It is deliberately separate from the mobilisation plan. The mobilisation plan describes what will be delivered, by whom, in what sequence and with what dependencies. The theory of change explains why King's believes those interventions will produce the intended change.

The theory should therefore cut across projects and deliverables rather than simply mirror Projects 2.1 to 2.4.

## North Star

**Makes the King's graduate premium real, visible, navigable and evidenced for every student.**

The North Star is the communicable destination for King's Edge. The theory of change sits underneath it and tests the causal argument required to make that claim credible.

A useful shorthand is:

> **The North Star is the thing we sell. The theory of change is the thing we test. The mobilisation plan is the thing we manage.**

## Current programme-level causal hypothesis

The current working hypothesis is:

> **If** King's makes the distinctive value of its education explicit, creates meaningful opportunities for students to develop through curriculum and wider experience, gives students the language and agency to navigate that development, and enables them to recognise and evidence what they have gained,
>
> **then** students will be better able to make purposeful choices, develop the capabilities and experiences that matter to their futures, and articulate the value of their King's education,
>
> **while** King's becomes better able to understand where its graduate premium is being created, where it needs strengthening, and how credibly to assert it.

This is a working proposition, not yet an agreed final theory of change.

## Emerging causal architecture

The North Star currently suggests five connected tests for the programme:

### Real

The graduate premium must exist in the educational experience itself. Students need meaningful access to curriculum, experiential learning, skills development, opportunities, relationships and other forms of development through which graduate value is created.

### Visible

Students, academics and the institution need to be able to recognise the value that is already being created. This includes making implicit or fragmented value more explicit and connecting evidence that currently sits in different parts of King's.

### Navigable

Students need to be able to make purposeful choices through the opportunities available to them, informed by their ambitions, interests, circumstances and evolving sense of purpose. King's also needs to be able to understand student demand and use it to shape the opportunity ecology.

### Evidenced

Students need credible evidence of their own development. Programmes need evidence of their contribution to graduate futures. King's needs sufficiently robust evidence to understand, strengthen and assert the institutional graduate premium.

### For every student

The programme must address scale, access and equity. The graduate premium should not depend principally on a student's prior knowledge, confidence, networks, discretionary time or ability to navigate fragmented provision unaided.

These five ideas should not yet be treated as a simple linear sequence. The theory of change needs to establish the causal relationships between them, including feedback loops.

## Emerging feedback loops

The developing propositions already suggest that King's Edge should operate as a learning system rather than a one-way production line.

Examples include:

- course-level graduate futures evidence can support external claims while also identifying where curriculum or provision needs strengthening;
- student purpose and demand can support individual navigation while also informing what opportunities King's commissions, develops or scales;
- a shared skills language can support student articulation while also helping programmes and opportunity providers identify capability development and gaps;
- institutional graduate premium evidence can support recruitment and reputation while also showing where the premium is strong, uneven or insufficiently evidenced;
- external validation can test whether King's assumptions about valuable capabilities, experiences and outcomes remain credible in the environments graduates enter.

These feedback loops need to be developed and tested as the theory of change matures.

## What needs to be developed next

The next stage should work backwards from the North Star and establish:

1. **Ultimate change:** what should be different for students, graduates and King's if the programme succeeds?
2. **Intermediate outcomes:** what observable changes need to happen before that ultimate change is possible?
3. **Mechanisms of change:** why should the interventions produce those outcomes?
4. **Outputs and capabilities:** what needs to exist or operate differently in King's?
5. **Deliverable contribution:** which deliverables create or enable each mechanism and outcome?
6. **Enabling conditions and assumptions:** what must be true for the causal chain to hold?
7. **Feedback loops:** how does evidence from students, programmes, destinations and external audiences improve the system over time?
8. **Measures:** what evidence would allow King's to test whether the theory is working?

## Relationship to the mobilisation plan

The mobilisation plan remains structured through:

**King's Edge -> Projects -> Deliverables -> Components -> Delivery steps**

That structure is designed for ownership and delivery management.

The theory of change should provide an additional programme-level layer. As it matures, each major mechanism and outcome should be traceable to the deliverables that contribute to it, and each significant deliverable should be able to explain which part of the theory of change it advances.

This will allow the programme to test not only whether work has been delivered, but whether the combined portfolio is plausibly producing the intended change.
