# King's Edge Theory of Change

**Status: Working theory for programme development and testing**  
**Last updated: 3 August 2026**

## Purpose of this document

This document holds the programme-level theory of change for King's Edge.

It is deliberately separate from the mobilisation plan. The mobilisation plan describes what will be delivered, by whom, in what sequence and with what dependencies. The theory of change explains why King's believes those interventions will produce the intended change.

The theory cuts across projects and deliverables rather than simply mirroring Projects 2.1 to 2.4.

## North Star

**Makes the King's graduate premium real, visible, navigable and evidenced for every student.**

The North Star is the communicable destination for King's Edge. The theory of change sits underneath it and makes the causal argument required to make that claim credible and testable.

A useful shorthand is:

> **The North Star is the thing we sell. The theory of change is the thing we test. The mobilisation plan is the thing we manage.**

The four qualities in the North Star describe the intended future state:

- **Real:** graduate value exists in the lived educational experience.
- **Visible:** students, staff and King's can recognise that value and understand how it is created.
- **Navigable:** students can make purposeful choices through the opportunities available to them.
- **Evidenced:** students and King's can substantiate development and use evidence to improve and communicate value.
- **For every student:** access to and realisation of the graduate premium is not systematically determined by course, background, confidence, prior networks, mode of study or discretionary time.

These qualities are not treated as a simple linear delivery sequence. The causal work is carried by the four mechanisms set out below.

## The problem

King's already creates substantial graduate value, but that value is too often implicit, fragmented and uneven. The nature and strength of the graduate experience can vary across courses and parts of the institution, while students' ability to access, navigate and capitalise on what is available can vary according to their background, circumstances and prior knowledge.

Curriculum, wider experience and graduate futures are not yet consistently connected into an intentional developmental journey. As a result, students vary in their ability to recognise, develop and articulate the value of their King's education, while King's cannot yet consistently understand where its graduate premium is strongest, where it is uneven, where it needs strengthening or how credibly to evidence it.

This problem has two related dimensions:

1. **The underlying experience is uneven.** Some students encounter richer, more intentional or better supported opportunities for development than others.
2. **The capacity to recognise and use the experience is uneven.** Students differ in the confidence, language, networks, information and available time they can draw on to navigate and capitalise on what King's offers.

King's Edge therefore has to improve both the substance of the graduate experience and students' ability to understand, shape and carry its value forward.

## Ultimate change

If King's Edge succeeds, students will experience a more intentional, connected and equitable education through which they can:

- develop disciplinary knowledge alongside intellectual, personal, social and professional capabilities;
- apply learning in meaningful contexts and build relationships with people, communities, organisations and challenges beyond the immediate classroom;
- make purposeful choices about their development while retaining space to explore and change direction;
- recognise how learning and development across different settings connect;
- substantiate what they know, can do and contribute; and
- carry a credible account of that development into their future lives.

King's will be better able to:

- understand where and how its graduate premium is being created;
- identify differences between courses, experiences and student groups;
- strengthen weaker or less equitable provision;
- align curriculum, wider opportunity, support and investment around student development;
- learn from students, graduates and external partners; and
- make credible, audience-specific claims about the value of a King's education.

## Programme-level causal hypothesis

> **If** King's strengthens the substance and equitable reach of the student experience, makes its developmental value explicit and connected, supports students to make purposeful choices, and enables development to be recognised and evidenced,
>
> **then** students will be better able to access meaningful opportunities, develop knowledge and capabilities, shape a coherent journey and articulate what they can contribute,
>
> **while** King's becomes better able to understand where its graduate premium is strong or uneven, improve the experience and assert its value with credibility.

Evidence from students, courses, graduate destinations and external audiences should flow back into educational design, opportunity development, support and investment. King's Edge should therefore operate as a learning system rather than a one-way production process.

## How the model works

The theory operates through four connected mechanisms:

1. **Create and strengthen value**
2. **Make value legible and connected**
3. **Enable purposeful student agency**
4. **Recognise, evidence and learn**

The mechanisms reinforce one another.

Improving the underlying experience creates more graduate value. Making that value legible helps students and staff recognise what the experience can enable. Purposeful agency helps students act on that understanding. Recognition and evidence allow students to carry their development forward and allow King's to learn where the experience should improve.

The test of **for every student** applies across all four mechanisms.

## Mechanism 1: Intentionally create and strengthen graduate value

King's uses evidence, shared educational principles and student insight to strengthen the substance, quality and equitable reach of the curriculum and wider experiences through which graduate value is created.

This mechanism begins from an honest recognition that graduate value is already being created across King's, but that its nature, strength and accessibility vary between courses, experiences and groups of students. Some of the work involves identifying, connecting and extending strong existing practice. Some involves making substantive improvements where students have fewer opportunities to develop or where the experience is not yet producing the outcomes King's intends.

### Evidence-led enhancement

Course teams, departments, faculties and institutional services gain a clearer understanding of the graduate value being created for different students. They use evidence from curriculum, student experience, graduate destinations, student ambitions and demographic patterns to identify strengths, gaps and uneven outcomes.

This evidence supports local judgement and proportionate action. Strong practice can be reinforced and shared, while weaker or less equitable provision can be addressed through normal curriculum, quality enhancement, commissioning and investment processes.

### Developmentally purposeful design

Curriculum, co-curricular and extra-curricular experiences are designed with greater intentionality about the development they enable.

Students encounter meaningful opportunities to apply disciplinary knowledge, exercise judgement, collaborate, create, contribute, build relationships, respond to uncertainty and engage with the world beyond the university. Experiences include appropriate support, challenge, reflection, feedback and opportunities to recognise development over time.

Shared frameworks, principles and reusable models help King's extend strong practice without reducing educational quality or imposing a single model across disciplines.

### Equitable reach and consistency

King's works to ensure that meaningful developmental opportunity is not concentrated in particular courses, faculties or parts of the student population.

Equity here means preserving disciplinary, personal and educational difference while ensuring that course, background, mode of study, confidence, prior networks or discretionary time do not systematically determine who can access and benefit from the experiences through which graduate value is created.

Student insight is used to understand who is participating, who is benefiting and what different students need. It also informs the opportunities King's develops, commissions, supports and scales.

### Causal claim

When students encounter high-quality, purposeful and appropriately supported opportunities to apply knowledge, develop capabilities, build relationships and exercise agency, they are more likely to experience durable intellectual, personal, social and professional development.

When King's combines this educational design with evidence about course performance, student experience, graduate destinations and patterns of participation, it can strengthen what works and intervene where value is weaker or less equitably realised.

This is how the King's graduate premium becomes more real in the lived experience of students.

### Expected intermediate outcomes

If this mechanism is working:

- students across King's have more consistent access to meaningful developmental experiences;
- courses and opportunities become more intentional about the forms of development they enable;
- students have more opportunities to apply disciplinary knowledge in authentic and consequential contexts;
- students develop stronger capabilities, agency, judgement, relationships, belonging and readiness to contribute;
- course teams and institutional leaders can identify and respond to weaker or uneven provision;
- differences in graduate value between courses and demographic groups reduce over time; and
- evidence from students and graduates continuously informs the improvement of provision.

## Mechanism 2: Make graduate value legible and connected

King's makes the developmental value of its curriculum and wider student experience more explicit, coherent and understandable, enabling students and staff to recognise how knowledge, capabilities, experiences, relationships and possible futures connect.

Graduate value does not become useful to students simply because it exists within a course or experience. Students need opportunities to notice what they are developing, understand how different parts of their education relate to one another and interpret those gains in relation to their own purposes and future possibilities.

At present, this sense-making is often left to the individual student. Courses, skills, co-curricular opportunities, extra-curricular experiences, academic achievement and graduate futures may be described through separate systems, frameworks and institutional languages. Students with greater confidence, prior knowledge or access to professional networks are better equipped to make connections between them.

This mechanism creates a shared architecture through which the value of a King's education can be understood, while preserving disciplinary difference, individual interpretation and appropriate limits on what King's can claim on a student's behalf.

### Make developmental possibilities explicit

Courses and wider opportunities explain more clearly what students may encounter, practise and develop through them.

This includes the disciplinary knowledge, skills, forms of judgement, relationships, experiences and future possibilities associated with different parts of the King's experience. Developmental intent becomes a more visible part of programme and opportunity design, communication, reflection and review.

These descriptions express what an experience is designed to enable. They do not assume that participation alone proves that every student has developed a particular capability.

### Establish a connected language and architecture

King's develops shared languages and structures that allow curriculum, wider experience, skills, purpose and graduate futures to connect.

A sufficiently rich skills language allows students and educators to describe development with greater precision while retaining disciplinary and individual difference. Courses and opportunities can show where capabilities might be encountered, practised and demonstrated. Information and digital systems can bring related elements of the student journey into a more coherent view.

This shared architecture reduces the burden on students to translate between disconnected institutional, disciplinary and external vocabularies. It also enables staff across King's to understand how their work contributes to a wider developmental journey.

### Support integrative student sense-making

Students are supported to interpret what their experiences mean for them.

Through reflection, dialogue and access to connected information, students can consider how their academic learning, skills, experiences, relationships and interests are developing over time. They can recognise patterns, make connections across different settings and relate their development to purposes and possible futures that may continue to change.

King's provides language, prompts and credible information, while the student retains ownership of the meaning they make and the claims they can substantiate. A shared architecture supports personal interpretation rather than replacing it with a standard institutional account of what every student should become.

### Causal claim

When the developmental possibilities within courses and wider opportunities are explicit, students are more likely to notice and engage intentionally with what those experiences can enable.

When students can connect learning and development across different contexts, they are better placed to transfer what they have learned, understand their own emerging capabilities and see how separate experiences contribute to a broader trajectory.

When King's uses a coherent language and information architecture, students are less dependent on prior confidence, networks or familiarity with institutional systems to recognise the value available to them.

This is how the King's graduate premium becomes visible as a connected and personally meaningful part of the student experience.

### Expected intermediate outcomes

If this mechanism is working:

- courses and opportunities describe their developmental value more clearly and credibly;
- students have a richer and more precise language for understanding what they are developing;
- students can identify connections between disciplinary learning, skills, wider experiences, relationships and future possibilities;
- students become more aware of their existing strengths and areas they may want to develop;
- staff understand more clearly how different parts of King's contribute to the wider student journey;
- incompatible or duplicative institutional frameworks reduce over time;
- students are less reliant on prior networks or professional familiarity to interpret the value of their experience; and
- King's develops a more coherent account of how its different educational and developmental offers fit together.

## Mechanism 3: Enable purposeful student agency

King's supports every student to explore and revisit what matters to them, make informed choices and shape a personally meaningful journey through their course and the wider King's experience.

Understanding the value available at King's does not by itself enable students to act. Students need space to consider their direction, relevant and manageable choices, and support that helps them move from reflection into action. They also need permission to remain uncertain and to revise their plans as they learn more about themselves and the world.

At present, the capacity to build a coherent journey can depend heavily on confidence, prior networks, institutional knowledge and the time available to search across fragmented provision. This mechanism makes purposeful navigation a supported part of the student experience.

### Make purpose a continuing conversation

From entry through to graduation, students are invited to consider what matters to them, what they hope to learn or contribute, where they need connection or support and how their direction is changing.

Purpose is understood broadly. It can include disciplinary interests, personal development, belonging, service, relationships, future work and contribution to the world beyond King's. Students are not required to arrive with a fixed destination. Exploration, ambiguity and changing direction remain legitimate parts of development.

### Connect purpose with relevant choices and support

Students can relate their evolving purposes to realistic opportunities within their course and the wider King's experience.

Connected information, carefully bounded digital tools and human relationships help students identify possible next steps. Recommendations and prompts make relevant options easier to see, while educators, advisers, peers and professional services provide judgement, challenge and care at meaningful moments.

The aim is to increase the student's capacity to choose rather than prescribe a single ideal journey.

### Reduce navigation advantage and practical barriers

King's simplifies the routes through which students discover, access and combine opportunities.

Information becomes easier to navigate, provision is better connected and opportunity design takes account of practical constraints such as timetable, location, mode of study, caring or paid work commitments and confidence entering unfamiliar spaces.

Where appropriate, recognition, credit, embedded provision and targeted support reduce dependence on discretionary time and prior institutional knowledge.

### Use student purpose to shape provision

Patterns in student ambitions, needs and choices help King's understand where provision is relevant, where demand is unmet and where some students remain poorly served.

This insight informs opportunity development, commissioning, communication, support and investment. Student purpose therefore becomes both an individual navigation mechanism and a source of institutional intelligence.

### Causal claim

When students can relate available choices to an evolving sense of purpose, and have the confidence and support to act, they are more likely to participate intentionally, sustain engagement and build a coherent developmental journey.

When navigation is simpler and practical barriers are addressed, access to developmental opportunity becomes less dependent on background, networks or familiarity with how universities work.

When King's understands what students are trying to achieve, it can shape the opportunity ecology more responsively and increase the relevance of what it offers.

This is how the King's graduate premium becomes navigable and how students gain greater agency over the value they realise from their education.

### Expected intermediate outcomes

If this mechanism is working:

- students can articulate evolving priorities, interests, needs and possible directions;
- students make more intentional choices about their learning and development;
- students connect their course with relevant wider opportunities, relationships and support;
- participation is better aligned with students' purposes and developmental needs;
- students report greater confidence, agency, belonging and ownership of their journey;
- demographic and course-level differences in opportunity discovery and participation reduce;
- fewer students depend on informal networks or chance encounters to find relevant opportunities; and
- King's uses student demand and unmet need to shape provision more responsively.

## Mechanism 4: Recognise, evidence and learn from development

King's enables students to capture, substantiate and communicate what they have developed, while responsibly aggregated evidence helps courses and the institution improve provision and assert the graduate premium with credibility.

Development can remain difficult to use when it is not recognised, recorded or connected to credible evidence. Academic transcripts provide an authoritative record of study and achievement, but they do not currently make the fuller knowledge, capabilities and development arising through a King's education sufficiently visible.

Students are often left to identify and translate this value independently. King's also lacks a sufficiently connected account of the development its courses and wider experiences enable. This mechanism creates trusted forms of recognition for students and a stronger evidence base for institutional learning.

### Maintain clear standards of evidence

King's distinguishes carefully between developmental intent and demonstrated development.

Courses and opportunities can state what students may encounter or practise. Credentials and formal records certify what King's is authorised to confirm. Students contribute evidence of what they have done and can substantiate. Personal claims remain owned by the student.

Clear distinctions between what is certified, evidenced and claimed protect the credibility of the whole model and avoid assuming that attendance or participation automatically proves capability.

### Build a connected and evolving record

Students can bring together academic achievement, skills, recognised experiences, institutional credentials and their own evidence in a coherent view that grows throughout their time at King's.

The record helps students see where development came from, recognise progress, identify areas they may want to strengthen and select evidence relevant to different purposes. It connects with the student's evolving sense of purpose and provides another route back into navigation and development.

### Support credible articulation

Students are supported to translate evidence into accounts of what they know, can do and contribute.

They can draw selectively on their record for applications, interviews, portfolios, profiles, further study, civic contribution or other future settings. The aim is not to impose a single King's story on every graduate. It is to help each student communicate a distinctive and credible account grounded in evidence.

### Turn evidence into institutional learning and assertion

Responsibly aggregated evidence gives courses, faculties and King's a clearer view of the knowledge, capabilities and experiences being developed across the institution.

Combined with graduate destinations, student insight, course-level evidence and external validation, this allows King's to identify strengths, unevenness and gaps in evidence. Findings feed back into curriculum enhancement, opportunity development, support and investment.

The same evidence supports credible and audience-specific accounts of the King's graduate premium for prospective students, current students, faculties, employers, partners, regulators and the wider public.

### Causal claim

When students can capture, substantiate and communicate their development, they are better able to carry the value of their education into future settings and make purposeful decisions about what to develop next.

When King's can aggregate evidence responsibly, it can understand where its graduate premium is strong, uneven or insufficiently evidenced. This enables improvement of the underlying experience and strengthens the credibility of external claims.

This is how the King's graduate premium becomes evidenced, portable and capable of supporting continuous learning.

### Expected intermediate outcomes

If this mechanism is working:

- students build an evolving and credible record of their learning and development;
- students understand the status and strength of different forms of evidence;
- students can construct relevant accounts of what they know, can do and contribute;
- courses and faculties gain stronger evidence of the value they create;
- King's can identify patterns in capability development, participation and outcomes;
- external claims are grounded in credible and appropriately qualified evidence;
- evidence informs curriculum, opportunity, support and investment decisions; and
- feedback from external audiences tests and refreshes King's assumptions about valuable development.

## Cross-cutting test: for every student

**For every student** is a distributional test applied across the whole theory of change.

It requires King's to ask:

- Who encounters high-quality developmental opportunity and who does not?
- Who can recognise and interpret the value of their experience?
- Who can navigate towards relevant opportunities and support?
- Whose development is recognised, evidenced and valued?
- Which courses or student groups experience weaker outcomes or carry a greater burden of self-navigation?
- Are interventions reducing or reproducing existing differences?

The programme should examine both average improvement and distribution. A stronger institutional graduate premium is insufficient if its benefits remain concentrated among students who already possess confidence, networks, time or familiarity with professional and institutional systems.

Equity does not require every student to follow the same path or acquire the same profile. It requires every student to have meaningful and supported opportunities to develop, make choices and substantiate the value of their individual King's experience.

## Feedback loops

King's Edge should operate as a learning system.

### Student purpose to provision

Insight into students' ambitions, needs, choices and barriers informs opportunity development, commissioning, support and communication.

### Course evidence to enhancement

Course-level graduate futures evidence supports credible articulation while also identifying where curriculum or student support could be strengthened.

### Skills and evidence to design

Patterns in student skills profiles and evidence help courses and opportunity providers understand what students are developing and where opportunities to practise or demonstrate capabilities are limited.

### Recognition to navigation

An evolving record of development helps students see progress, identify gaps and decide what they want to pursue next.

### Graduate outcomes to improvement

Graduate destinations, progression, qualitative accounts and longer-term outcomes test whether the experience is translating into meaningful futures for different student groups.

### External validation to institutional learning

Employers, alumni, partners, professions and wider communities test whether King's assumptions about valuable knowledge, capabilities and experiences remain credible in the settings graduates enter.

### Institutional evidence to external assertion

Evidence supports recruitment, reputation and accountability, while scrutiny from those audiences exposes weak, uneven or unsupported claims that require further work.

## Critical assumptions and enabling conditions

The causal model depends on several assumptions that should be tested rather than treated as settled facts.

### Educational quality

The experiences being made visible and navigable are sufficiently purposeful, challenging and supported to create meaningful development.

### Student participation

Students have realistic opportunities, time and motivation to engage, and provision is designed around the circumstances of different groups.

### Human relationships

Digital tools and shared frameworks are accompanied by human judgement, dialogue, care and challenge where these are needed.

### Academic and disciplinary ownership

Common institutional architecture leaves room for academic judgement, disciplinary identity and local adaptation.

### Data quality and governance

Student, course, participation and outcome data are sufficiently accurate, connected and responsibly governed to support decisions without generating false precision or inappropriate surveillance.

### Credible evidence standards

King's maintains distinctions between participation, developmental possibility, student evidence and formally certified achievement.

### Organisational capacity

Courses, faculties and services have the time, capability, incentives and authority to act on evidence and coordinate across institutional boundaries.

### Opportunity supply

King's can develop, commission or connect sufficient high-quality opportunities to respond to student purposes and identified gaps.

### Equity by design

Equity is considered in educational design, operating models, communication, digital systems, timetabling and investment rather than evaluated only after implementation.

### External relevance without narrow instrumentalism

Dialogue with employers and external partners informs the model while preserving the wider intellectual, personal, civic and social purposes of higher education.

## Relationship to the mobilisation plan

The mobilisation plan remains structured through:

**King's Edge -> Projects -> Deliverables -> Components -> Delivery steps**

That structure is designed for ownership and delivery management.

The theory of change provides an additional programme-level layer. Each major mechanism and outcome should become traceable to the deliverables that contribute to it, and each significant deliverable should be able to explain which part of the theory it advances.

The next mapping stage should establish:

1. which mechanisms each deliverable activates or enables;
2. which intermediate outcomes it is expected to influence;
3. what assumptions and dependencies sit between delivery and benefit realisation;
4. where multiple deliverables must work together for an outcome to be plausible;
5. where the current portfolio leaves a causal gap; and
6. what evidence would test contribution rather than merely confirm completion.

This will allow the programme to test not only whether work has been delivered, but whether the combined portfolio is plausibly producing the intended change.

## Current validation questions

The working theory should now be tested with students, academic colleagues, professional services, institutional leaders and external partners.

Key questions include:

- Does the problem statement reflect genuine differences in both underlying experience and students' capacity to realise its value?
- Are the four mechanisms distinct enough to be useful while still showing how they reinforce one another?
- Does the model give sufficient weight to disciplinary knowledge and the broader purposes of higher education?
- Does purposeful agency feel enabling rather than prescriptive?
- Are the proposed evidence standards credible and understandable?
- Does the equity test expose meaningful differences rather than treating inclusion as a general aspiration?
- Which causal links are supported by existing evidence and which need to be tested through pilots?
- Which outcomes can King's influence directly and which depend substantially on external economic or social conditions?
- Where does the mobilisation portfolio currently underinvest in a mechanism or enabling condition?

The theory should be revised as proposition development, delivery design, pilots and evidence provide stronger answers to these questions.
